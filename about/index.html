Why I started Halfspaces

I want to help people get their time back for what matters: one more project closed, or one more hour with family.

I’ve seen what that time can do. When I was leading a research team, AI and automation shaved hours off my routine and project work, and I spent them where no tool could: catching up with my team, checking in on their development, being a more present manager and mentor. I didn’t realise then how fortunate I was to be in that position.

Plenty of people running SMEs can see what AI and automation could do for them. Working out what would help is another job on an already long list; even choosing a course takes time, and most are built for a broad audience. Halfspaces is for them: people whose work could run better, who don’t have the time, the budget or anyone to show them where to start.

I’m convinced the only way to help them is to live their working lives with them for a while. Fourteen years as a researcher taught me to read the room and ask the questions that get to the real problem, and since April 2026 I’ve tinkered enough with AI and automation to believe I can be useful. The aim is to find the bottlenecks together and fix them with whatever suits the business, which isn’t always AI, so the time keeps coming back after I’ve gone.

Halfspaces is named after a football term. The half-spaces are the channels between the middle of the pitch and the wings, where a well-placed player is hard to mark and can find the room to set up a teammate. That’s the part I want Halfspaces to play in a business: finding the room that’s already there, and turning it into time for what matters.

Nigel LinFounder, Halfspaces

Building Podviz

A visual companion that plays alongside a podcast, drawing what’s being said. Built in a day and shelved before it was ever tested with listeners.

July–November 2026 · one self-contained HTML file · built with Claude, reviewed by Codex · shelved

Pause, rewind, search

It came from one investing podcast episode that I kept pausing, rewinding and searching my way through. So the idea was a screen that plays in sync with the episode and draws what’s being said, with no words of its own. You can only touch it when it’s paused, to keep it from competing with the listening.

The prototype took one session on 24 July: a single HTML file with no dependencies, seven animated scenes covering roughly the first eighteen minutes of one episode, kept in time by a timer. Tapping a quoted line re-syncs it after the ads.

There’s no screenshot here. The prototype is built on a real episode’s words, and that episode isn’t mine to publish.

Three of the seven scenes misled

The same day, I asked Codex whether the scenes taught anything or just put pictures next to the audio. Its audit scored them: three taught, one only decorated, and three misled, drawing something that pointed the listener the wrong way. All three were fixed that afternoon.

A podcast has no single timeline

The engineering surprise was that the same episode had different timings on different platforms. One platform’s copy ran about ninety seconds behind the video version, so any fixed sync was wrong somewhere. Listening through the microphone to find your place would fail for anyone using earphones. I concluded it would need its own player.

Kill criteria, written first

Before any test, the criteria went on paper. Five listeners. Carry on only if at least three of the five would choose it again, their understanding comes out at least a little better, and no more than one finds it distracting. Otherwise park it, and stop polishing the animations. Codex added a warning I needed: it mustn’t become a comfortable escape from the harder test on the project it grew out of.

The test that never happened

I never ran it. Two months later I still hadn’t recruited anyone.

A separate reading example in September found little advantage over a general AI assistant given the same sources, though it never tested the listening prototype itself. When the project Podviz grew out of was paused in October, nothing depended on it any more, and in November I closed the test without running it.

What AI did, and what it didn’t

Claude built the prototype and checked it in a browser frame by frame; Codex vetted the idea and audited the teaching; the September example was built by an OpenAI model and reviewed by Claude. Mine were the idea, the rule that it only responds when paused, the kill criteria and the decision to shelve it. The next step was mine: recruiting five listeners to compare the audio alone with audio plus pictures.
